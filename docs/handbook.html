Photo Archive

This app copies your job photos out of ServiceTitan and saves them as ordinary folders on your own computer, so you keep them forever — even after you stop paying for ServiceTitan.

Everything below is written step by step. If you can use email, you can use this.

01 What this app does

In one sentence: it gets your pictures out.

Every time your technicians take photos on a job, those pictures go into ServiceTitan. They stay there as long as you keep paying for ServiceTitan. If you ever stop — or switch to different software — the pictures go away with it. ServiceTitan has no button that downloads them all.

This app is that button. It logs into your ServiceTitan, finds every photo, and saves them into folders named after your customers and the dates of the jobs.

✓

It cannot break anything. This app only reads from ServiceTitan. It can't change, delete, or move anything in your account. Think of it as taking photocopies — the originals stay exactly where they are.

02 Opening the app

Same as opening any website.

Open your web browser

Chrome, Edge, Safari — whichever you normally use.

Type the address you were given

It looks like photos.yourcompany.com or localhost:3100/app. Whoever set this up for you will have sent it. Press Enter.

Bookmark it

Press Ctrl + D so you never have to type it again.

!

Nothing loads? The app runs on a computer that has to be switched on. If the page won't open, that computer or server is off — contact whoever set it up. Nothing is lost; it just isn't running right now.

Tell the app whose photos it's fetching.

Top right of the screen.

Type your company name

Exactly as you'd write it on an invoice.

This is the day your ServiceTitan subscription ends. The app counts down to it on every screen, because after that date the photos can't be fetched at all. Leave it blank if you're not leaving ServiceTitan.

05 Connecting to ServiceTitan

The one fiddly part. Do it once and never again.

The app needs permission to read your ServiceTitan. That permission comes as four codes you copy from ServiceTitan and paste into the app.

✓

Don't do this alone if you'd rather not. Whoever manages ServiceTitan for you — your office manager, your IT person, or ServiceTitan's own support — can get these four codes in about ten minutes. Forward them this section.

The four codes

What it's calledWhat it looks like
Tenant IDA long number, like 1701031075. This identifies your company.
Client IDA long string of letters and numbers.
Client SecretAnother long string. Treat it like a password — never email it.
App KeyOne more string, starting with letters.

Where they come from

Someone with ServiceTitan admin access creates an "application" inside ServiceTitan's developer area, and it hands back those four codes. The screens change from time to time, so use this as a map rather than exact directions:

Sign in to ServiceTitan's developer portal

developer.servicetitan.io — with a ServiceTitan admin account.

Create a new app

Give it a name like "Photo Archive". It will show you a Client ID and Client Secret.

Turn on the permissions it needs

The app needs read access to Job Planning & Management, CRM, and — this is the one everybody forgets — Forms.

Approve the app inside ServiceTitan

In ServiceTitan itself (not the developer site), under Settings → Integrations → API Application Access, approve the app you just made. That page also shows your Tenant ID and gives you the App Key.

⚠

Forms permission is the difference between photos and no photos. ServiceTitan stores job photos in a section called Forms. Without that permission the app still works, still downloads your job records — and brings back zero pictures. If you only remember one thing from this page, remember this.

Putting them in

Open your company, then the Settings tab

You'll land on 01 Connection.

Leave "Which ServiceTitan" on Production

Production means your real data. Sandbox is a practice version with made-up jobs in it.

Paste the four codes into the four boxes
Click Save changes, then Test connection

If it worked you'll see something like "Connected to their production account — 75,450 jobs visible." That number is how many jobs you have. Seeing it means everything is right.

✓

Your codes are safe. They're scrambled before being stored, and the app never shows them again — even to you. If you need to change one, just type the new one over the top.

06 Getting one job's photos

For when someone calls and needs pictures from a specific job.

Click the One job tab
Type the job number

The number you see on the job in ServiceTitan. For example 89496.

Choose what you want

Photos only is right nearly every time. The other choices add invoices and signed estimates, or the job's written records.

Click Look up

You'll see the customer's name, their address, and how many photos exist. Check the name is who you expected before going further.

Click Download photos

A zip file lands in your Downloads folder within a few seconds.

07 Getting all your photos

The big one. Start it, walk away, come back later.

This fetches every photo from every job in a date range. For a company with years of history it can take days — not because the app is slow, but because it's being deliberately gentle so your dispatchers aren't slowed down while it runs.

Click the Photo archive tab
Set the dates

From and To. It starts on the last seven years, which for most companies is everything. Leave Match jobs by on Date completed.

Click Check size first

Takes about a minute and downloads nothing. It tells you how many jobs, roughly how many photos, how big, and how long. Read it before committing.

Click Start archive

Now close the page if you like. The work happens on the server, not in your browser. Turn your computer off and it keeps going.

Come back and download the months

The app splits your history into one file per month. Each month gets a Download link the moment it's finished — you don't wait for the whole thing to grab January.

08 Opening what you downloaded

Ordinary folders. Nothing special needed.

Each download is a zip file — a folder that's been squeezed into one file so it travels in one piece. On Windows: right-click it, choose Extract All, click Extract. On a Mac: double-click it.

Inside, it's organised by job:

Titanz-Plumbing-photos-2026-07.zip
  88801 - Dana Whitfield - 2026-07-03/
    Dana Whitfield - 2026-07-03 - 001.jpg
    Dana Whitfield - 2026-07-03 - 002.jpg
    Dana Whitfield - 2026-07-03 - 003.jpg
  88802 - Marcus Reyes - 2026-07-19/
    Marcus Reyes - 2026-07-19 - 001.jpg
  README.txt   ← a note listing anything that couldn't be fetched

Each folder is one job: job number, customer name, date. Each photo is named the same way, so a picture still makes sense months later when it's sitting on its own in an email.

✓

These are normal picture files. Any computer opens them, forever, with no software and no subscription. That's the whole point.

Always open README.txt. If anything couldn't be fetched, it's listed there by name. An empty warnings list means you got everything.

09 Sending photos to someone

An adjuster, a customer, a lawyer.

Small job, a few photos?

Attach them straight to an email. Anything under about 20 MB is fine.

A whole month, or several?

Email won't take it. Upload the folder to Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive, then send the person a link. Drag the extracted folder into the browser window — that's all "uploading" means.

Keep your own copy somewhere else

One copy is not a backup. Put the archive on an external drive as well as in the cloud. These photos can't be fetched again after your ServiceTitan account closes.

10 Cleaning up space

Archives are big. Delete them once you've saved them somewhere safe.

Finished archives sit on the server taking up room. Once you've downloaded a month and saved it properly, you can remove it.

One month at a time

Click Delete on that month's row. The rest of the archive stays.

A whole archive

Click Delete at the top of the archive. Every month in it goes.

Confirm when it asks

It tells you exactly what's about to go and how big it is. Nothing deletes on a single click.

⚠

Deleting here does not touch ServiceTitan. It only removes the copy on the server. But if your ServiceTitan account has already closed, that copy might be the only one left — so make sure you've saved it somewhere before you delete.

Anything you forget about is removed automatically after 14 days, so the server can't fill up on its own.

11 When something goes wrong

The handful of things that actually happen, and what to do.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
"Needs credentials" The four ServiceTitan codes haven't been entered yet. Go to Settings → Connection. See section 05.
Test connection says invalid_client The Client ID or Client Secret is wrong, or was changed in ServiceTitan. Get a fresh Client Secret from the developer portal and paste it in again.
Everything works but no photos come back Almost always the Forms permission is missing. Have your ServiceTitan admin add Forms access, then re-approve the app. See section 05.
"No ServiceTitan job found" Either a typo, or the app is pointed at Sandbox instead of Production. Check the number, then check Settings → Connection says Production.
"Archive running" and the button won't click One archive is already going for this company. Wait for it, or click Stop and start again with the dates you meant.
"Interrupted by a server restart" The server rebooted mid-archive. Months already finished are still fine. Start a new archive for the dates that didn't finish.
A month says "Failed" That month hit a problem; the others are unaffected. Run a new archive covering just that month.
It's going very slowly It's designed to. Slow keeps ServiceTitan fast for your staff. Leave it. Only raise Settings → Speed if you're told it's safe.
The page won't load at all The server is switched off. Contact whoever set the app up. Nothing is lost.
✓

Nothing you click in this app can damage ServiceTitan. It has no ability to write, edit, or delete anything there. The worst case is a download that fails, and you simply run it again.

12 Words explained

Terms you'll bump into, in plain English.

Zip file
A folder squeezed into a single file so it's easy to move around. Right-click → Extract All to open it.
Tenant
ServiceTitan's word for your company's account. Your Tenant ID is your company's number.
Credentials
The four codes that let the app read your ServiceTitan. Like a key cut for one specific door.
Cutover date
The day your ServiceTitan subscription ends. After it, your photos can't be fetched by anyone.
Archive
One batch of photos fetched in one go, split into monthly files.
Manifest / README
The note inside each zip listing anything that couldn't be fetched. Your proof nothing went missing quietly.
Sandbox
A practice copy of ServiceTitan with fake jobs. Useful for testing, useless for real photos.
Server
The always-on computer running this app. It keeps working when your own computer is off.
